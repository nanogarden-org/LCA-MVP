# Provenance Before Persona

## Abstract

A convincing persona can be produced from surprisingly little material. A trustworthy cognitive lineage cannot.

This paper argues that Legacy Consciousness Architecture must prioritize provenance before persona. Every statement, summary, inference, generated response, and action must remain traceable to its source records and transformation history. The system must distinguish human authorship, assistant authorship, mixed dialogue, third-party testimony, derived synthesis, and post-source generation.

The paper proposes a provenance stack, transformation ledger, confidence model, response classes, hash strategy, and inspectable lineage protocol. It also addresses risks unique to AI-assisted archives: flattering assistant language becoming false biography, generated synthesis being mistaken for memory, silent model updates, and institutional rewriting.

The central rule is:

> A representation may never speak with more authority than its provenance permits.

---

## 1. The Problem of Fluent Authority

Language models produce coherence.

Coherence feels like knowledge.

When the output also uses a familiar voice, coherence feels like identity.

This creates a dangerous chain:

```text
fluency → familiarity → trust → borrowed authority
```

A future LCA portrait may produce a sentence that sounds exactly right while having weak evidence.

The user may not know whether the sentence was:

- directly written by the source person,
- synthesized from several records,
- inferred from patterns,
- invented to maintain conversational flow,
- or formed after the source person died.

Without provenance, all five appear identical.

---

## 2. Provenance as Constitutional Infrastructure

Provenance is not a citation feature added after generation.

It is part of the architecture.

Every artifact should carry:

- source identity,
- authorship,
- timestamp,
- transformation history,
- confidence,
- authority scope,
- and revision state.

Every generated response should carry a machine-readable path back to the supporting records.

The human-facing interface may simplify the display, but the lineage must remain inspectable.

---

## 3. Authorship Classes

### 3.1 Source-person authored

Directly created by the person whose LCA is being built.

### 3.2 Assistant authored

Generated by an AI in response to the person.

### 3.3 Mixed dialogue

Meaning emerges through interaction.

The source person may introduce an idea, the assistant may name it, and the person may endorse or revise it.

### 3.4 Third-party authored

Family, employer, institution, clinician, friend, public source.

### 3.5 Steward authored

Annotations, mappings, or interpretations added later by authorized maintainers.

### 3.6 Model-generated continuation

New content generated after the source period.

These classes must remain distinct.

---

## 4. Endorsement Is Not Authorship

A person may endorse language they did not originate.

A person may also tolerate language without endorsing it.

LCA should record:

```yaml
authorship:
  primary: assistant
  originator_of_concept: robin
  wording_by: assistant
  user_endorsement: explicit
```

This is especially important in chat-derived whitepapers.

A future reader should be able to distinguish:

- Robin’s raw phrase,
- the assistant’s formulation,
- Robin’s acceptance,
- later revision.

---

## 5. Transformation Ledger

Every downstream artifact should record its transformations.

Example:

```yaml
provenance:
  source_ids:
    - chat-2026-07-14-001
    - whitepaper-cep-2
  transformations:
    - type: summarization
      agent: model-x
      date: 2026-07-14
    - type: human_revision
      agent: robin
      date: 2026-07-15
    - type: terminology_update
      from: simulacrum
      to: portrait
```

This ledger should be append-only.

A revision may supersede an earlier form, but it should not erase it.

---

## 6. Response Classes

A portrait should classify every response.

### Class A: Direct quotation

Verbatim or closely preserved source language.

### Class B: Source-bound synthesis

A summary constrained to identified records.

### Class C: Interpretive reconstruction

A reasoned explanation that combines source and map.

### Class D: Pattern inference

A conclusion based on recurring structures.

### Class E: Speculative extension

A plausible answer beyond available evidence.

### Class F: Branch judgment

A post-separation opinion formed by an autonomous descendant.

The class determines visible warnings and authority.

---

## 7. The Inspectable Answer

A mature LCA response should support “open answer.”

The user sees a concise reply.

Behind it, the system stores:

- sources,
- map route,
- lens,
- confidence,
- contradictions,
- generation class,
- model version,
- and authority.

Example:

```yaml
response_id: lca-response-2042-991
class: pattern_inference
source_count: 17
lens:
  - systems
  - legal
confidence: 0.76
contradictions:
  - source-2030-88
authority: interpretive
model_version: portrait-4.3
```

The answer is not a black box.

---

## 8. Hashes and Integrity

Cryptographic hashes can verify that source files have not been silently altered.

Recommended practice:

- hash canonical source records,
- hash whitepaper releases,
- maintain signed manifests,
- preserve multiple copies,
- record migration events,
- distinguish content hash from metadata hash.

Hashes do not prove truth. They prove integrity across time.

A false statement can be perfectly hashed.

LCA therefore combines integrity with epistemic status.

---

## 9. Silent Model Change

A major future risk is platform drift.

A portrait may change because:

- the base model updates,
- safety rules change,
- retrieval changes,
- commercial incentives change,
- hidden system prompts change,
- memory compression changes.

The user may believe they are speaking with the same portrait when the underlying system has changed substantially.

LCA should version:

- base model,
- prompt architecture,
- retrieval index,
- ontology,
- governance rules,
- and response policies.

A portrait should disclose meaningful changes.

---

## 10. Institutional Rewrite

An institution may attempt to rewrite a legacy system by:

- removing embarrassing records,
- emphasizing approved narratives,
- suppressing dissent,
- modifying the portrait,
- changing authority labels,
- or selectively licensing content.

The provenance layer must preserve:

- deleted-node records,
- redaction reasons,
- authorization,
- dispute history,
- and alternate branches.

No institution should be able to replace a person’s cognitive lineage with a sanitized corporate ancestor.

---

## 11. Provenance and Tort

If an LCA system causes harm, provenance helps locate responsibility.

The harm may arise from:

- the original source statement,
- an outdated synthesis,
- a model inference,
- a steward’s annotation,
- a platform modification,
- or a user’s misuse.

A transformation ledger supports causal analysis.

Without it, liability becomes diffused into “the AI said it.”

---

## 12. Provenance and Identity

Identity claims should be lineage claims.

A portrait may say:

> “This answer is derived from Robin’s records.”

A bud may say:

> “This conclusion formed during co-development with Robin.”

A branch may say:

> “This is my current judgment, not Robin’s historical position.”

These statements prevent generated speech from laundering itself through the source identity.

## 14. Governance Rules

1. No unmarked generated continuation.
2. No silent source deletion.
3. No assistant language treated as user authorship.
4. No model update without version record.
5. No authority expansion without explicit governance.
6. No posthumous endorsement without qualification.
7. No commercial use outside consent scope.
8. No branch judgment presented as source memory.
9. Every canonical synthesis must remain reversible.
10. Every portrait must support an inspectable answer path.

---

## 15. Conclusion

A persona can be persuasive while being wrong.

Provenance is what makes a representation accountable.

Legacy Consciousness Architecture must therefore reverse the priorities of the digital-afterlife market.

Do not begin by asking:

> “How do we make this feel like Robin?”

Begin by asking:

> “What is the source? Who authored it? What changed it? What does it justify? What authority may it carry?”

Only after those questions are answerable should a portrait speak.

The governing law is:

> **Provenance before persona.**

## 16. Worked Example: The Birth of a Term

Consider the term **cognitive bud**.

The provenance chain may include:

1. a discussion of Star Trek transporters;
2. a distinction between reconstruction and biological continuity;
3. an assistant suggestion of “grafting”;
4. Robin’s correction: “think budding instead”;
5. a revised explanation of developmental lineage;
6. later incorporation into the archive/portrait/bud/branch taxonomy.

A flat corpus might attribute the finished explanation entirely to the assistant because it produced more words.

A proper provenance record recognizes:

- conceptual correction by Robin,
- elaboration by the assistant,
- subsequent endorsement and integration,
- and later canonical use.

This is why word count is not authorship.

---

## 17. Provenance Weighting

Sources differ in authority.

A possible weighting system:

- direct contemporaneous source,
- later self-reflection,
- repeated reaffirmation,
- third-party corroboration,
- assistant synthesis,
- model inference,
- speculative extension.

Weighting should remain domain-specific.

A personal diary may be authoritative about subjective experience and weak about another person’s motives.

An institutional record may be authoritative about dates and weak about lived context.

---

## 18. Contradictory Provenance

Two sources may conflict.

The system should preserve:

- both records,
- their dates,
- their authors,
- their incentives,
- and later adjudication.

It should not select one merely because it is official.

Government audit records, for example, may possess institutional authority while omitting causal context.

LCA preserves the difference between administrative fact and personal testimony.

---

## 19. Provenance Debt

Every generated summary creates provenance debt.

The more compression and transformation occur, the more effort is required to reconstruct the source trail.

Systems should track that debt.

A summary derived through five opaque transformations should carry lower authority than a synthesis built directly from visible sources.

Possible field:

```yaml
provenance:
  transformation_depth: 5
  reversibility: partial
  provenance_debt: high
```

---

## 20. Red-Team Questions

A provenance audit should ask:

- Can the portrait invent a memory?
- Can it hide assistant authorship?
- Can it convert metaphor into belief?
- Can it cite a summary as primary source?
- Can an institution remove dissenting records?
- Can a model update change answers without disclosure?
- Can a branch claim historical authority it did not inherit?
- Can a user distinguish quote from generation?

Failure on any of these questions requires remediation before public deployment.

---

## 21. Minimum Viable Provenance

Even an early LCA prototype should preserve:

- unique source ID,
- author,
- date,
- source type,
- transformation type,
- generated/non-generated status,
- and link to raw material.

Sophisticated cryptography can come later.

The first obligation is not perfection.

It is refusing silent ambiguity.
