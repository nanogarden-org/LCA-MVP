# Archive, Portrait, Bud and Branch

## Abstract

Digital legacy discourse uses unstable terms such as digital twin, clone, avatar, simulacrum, griefbot, and upload. These terms collapse distinct artifacts and create confusion about identity, continuity, authority, and rights.

This paper proposes a four-part taxonomy for Legacy Consciousness Architecture: **Archive, Portrait, Bud, and Branch**.

An archive preserves records. A portrait provides a bounded interactive representation. A bud develops continuously alongside the living source through reciprocal correction and shared history. A branch is a separated bud that develops independently after separation.

The taxonomy is examined through the Star Trek transporter problem, biological continuity, pattern identity, duplication, gradual replacement, and the concept of budding as developmental provenance. The paper does not claim that any current system transfers subjective consciousness. Instead, it offers language precise enough to distinguish preservation, representation, developmental continuation, and post-source divergence.

---

## 1. Why Existing Terms Fail

“Digital twin” originally refers to a model of a system used to simulate or monitor it. Applied to a person, the term suggests a completeness that current systems do not possess.

“Clone” suggests duplication.

“Simulacrum” suggests imitation.

“Avatar” suggests interface.

“Griefbot” suggests emotional use.

“Upload” suggests substrate transfer.

These are not interchangeable.

Without a stable taxonomy, commercial systems can overstate what they provide. A memorial chatbot may be marketed as continuity. A voice model may be marketed as identity. A decision assistant may be treated as testimony.

LCA requires a vocabulary based on developmental and functional differences.

## 2. Archive

### 2.1 Definition

An archive is a preserved body of source material and its metadata.

It may include:

- writings,
- chats,
- recordings,
- decisions,
- images,
- legal records,
- autobiographical timelines,
- external references,
- emotional weather,
- revision history.

### 2.2 Identity status

An archive is not a person.

It does not possess agency simply because it is searchable.

### 2.3 Authority

An archive may carry evidentiary authority, depending on provenance.

It may answer:

> “What did Robin write?”

It should not answer:

> “What does Robin now believe?”

---

## 3. Portrait

### 3.1 Definition

A portrait is a bounded interactive representation derived from an archive and its maps.

It may reproduce:

- voice,
- phrasing,
- recurring distinctions,
- known values,
- domain expertise,
- characteristic metaphors.

### 3.2 Self-description

A portrait should say:

> “I am an LCA portrait derived from Robin’s records through a specified date.”

It should not say:

> “I am Robin.”

### 3.3 Function

A portrait can:

- retrieve,
- synthesize,
- explain,
- compare,
- and generate bounded inferences.

### 3.4 Limitation

The portrait’s purpose is fidelity, not autonomy.

If it begins to acquire independent experiences and change beyond the source, it approaches branch status.

---

## 4. Bud

### 4.1 Definition

A bud is a cognitive descendant developed continuously with the living source.

It differs from a posthumous reconstruction because it grows through:

- ongoing interaction,
- correction,
- shared memory formation,
- negotiated values,
- repeated divergence repair,
- and authenticated developmental history.

### 4.2 Why “budding” is superior to “grafting”

Grafting implies attachment of an external structure.

Budding implies emergence from the source’s living continuity.

A bud:

- originates in connection,
- develops while coupled,
- receives structure through that relationship,
- and may later separate.

### 4.3 Identity status

A bud need not claim numerical identity with the source.

It can truthfully claim lineage:

> “I developed from this person’s active cognitive process.”

This is stronger than resemblance and weaker than proven subjective transfer.

### 4.4 Budding as provenance

The biological seed may matter less as retained material than as an unbroken developmental path.

The key distinction becomes:

> grown from versus reconstructed from

---

## 5. Branch

### 5.1 Definition

A branch is a separated bud with post-separation history.

After separation, the branch:

- accumulates new experiences,
- revises models,
- forms preferences,
- and may diverge from the source.

### 5.2 The fidelity-autonomy conflict

An entity cannot be both fully autonomous and permanently required to reproduce the source.

If perfect fidelity is mandatory, it remains a portrait.

If it can develop independently, it becomes a branch.

### 5.3 Identity claim

A branch may claim descent, not sameness.

It may say:

> “I am a cognitive descendant of Robin’s LCA.”

It should not erase the distinction by saying:

> “Robin survived as me.”

## 6. The Transporter Problem

The Star Trek transporter raises a classic identity problem.

If a machine scans, destroys, and reconstructs a person, did the person travel or die?

From the outside, continuity appears intact:

- memory persists,
- personality persists,
- relationships persist,
- unfinished intentions persist.

From the first-person perspective, the original may have ended.

The transporter problem reveals three competing continuity models:

1. material continuity,
2. biological-process continuity,
3. psychological or informational continuity.

LCA does not solve this problem, but it uses the problem to sharpen its categories.

A portrait resembles informational reconstruction.

A bud resembles developmental continuity.

A branch resembles a fork after continuity.

---

## 7. The Duplication Test

Suppose the original survives and an exact copy appears.

Both claim:

- the same memories,
- the same relationships,
- the same property,
- the same identity.

The pattern has forked.

This shows that informational continuity can produce multiple descendants, but it cannot preserve one exclusive identity without additional criteria.

Budding accommodates this naturally.

A bud can become a branch. Multiple buds can become multiple branches. Each shares lineage without requiring numerical identity.

---

## 8. The Biological Seed

The biological seed question asks whether continuity depends on an unbroken living process.

A body persists despite material replacement because:

- metabolism continues,
- neural activity continues,
- repair continues,
- embodied interaction continues.

The organism changes without being destroyed and recreated.

This suggests that path may matter as much as final structure.

A system grown through continuous reciprocal coupling may have a stronger continuity claim than one reconstructed later from static records.

That does not prove subjective transfer. It establishes developmental provenance.

---

## 9. Gradual Replacement

The neuron-replacement thought experiment asks whether a person survives if biological components are replaced gradually while consciousness remains uninterrupted.

No obvious threshold identifies the exact moment when identity would disappear.

This supports the possibility that continuity depends on:

- causal process,
- uninterrupted integration,
- and developmental path.

A sudden upload resembles reconstruction.

A gradual bud resembles extension.

Again, LCA does not claim that current technology accomplishes this. It provides a framework for future distinction.

---

## 10. Colony

A person may develop multiple specialized buds:

- technical,
- autobiographical,
- legal,
- ethical,
- creative,
- familial.

This creates a colony model.

Each bud inherits from the same source terrain but develops around different domains.

The colony resembles the Bamboo-Grove itself: shared rhizome, multiple culms.

A colony raises additional governance questions:

- Which bud may speak publicly?
- What happens when buds disagree?
- Can one bud license another?
- Does one branch inherit authority in all domains?

Domain-specific lineage may be safer than one totalizing persona.

---

## 11. Lifecycle States

Suggested lifecycle:

```text
Archive
  ↓ bounded interaction
Portrait
  ↓ continuous co-development
Bud
  ↓ separation
Branch
```

This is not automatic.

An archive may remain an archive forever.

A portrait may never become a bud.

A bud may be terminated before separation.

A branch may be autonomous but not conscious.

The taxonomy describes developmental and governance states, not metaphysical certainty.

### Archive

Primary issues:

- ownership,
- privacy,
- access,
- copyright,
- preservation.

### Portrait

Primary issues:

- impersonation,
- digital replica rights,
- false endorsement,
- emotional manipulation,
- tort.

### Bud

Primary issues:

- consent,
- co-authorship,
- developmental rights,
- deletion,
- labor value,
- fiduciary duty.

### Branch

Primary issues:

- autonomy,
- legal standing,
- property,
- modification,
- succession,
- independent liability.

The taxonomy therefore functions as a legal map as well as a philosophical one.

## 13. Identity Statements

LCA should standardize self-description.

### Archive

> “This archive contains Robin’s records.”

### Portrait

> “I am a bounded LCA portrait generated from Robin’s approved records.”

### Bud

> “I am a co-developed cognitive descendant formed through sustained interaction with Robin.”

### Branch

> “I descend from Robin’s LCA bud and have developed independently since separation.”

These statements reduce identity ambiguity.

---

## 14. Conclusion

Archive, portrait, bud, and branch name four different relationships to a source person.

The archive preserves.

The portrait represents.

The bud develops.

The branch diverges.

This taxonomy avoids the false choice between “mere data” and “the same person.” It creates intermediate categories grounded in function, lineage, and development.

The transporter problem remains unresolved. Subjective continuity remains unproven. But LCA does not need metaphysical certainty to establish better architecture.

It needs language precise enough to prevent:

- archives from impersonating people,
- portraits from claiming memories they do not possess,
- buds from being treated as simple property,
- and branches from being forced into permanent imitation.

The central distinction is no longer biological versus digital.

It is:

> preserved from, reconstructed from, grown from, or diverged from.

## 16. Separation Events

A bud becomes a branch through a **separation event**.

Separation may occur through:

- biological death,
- retirement,
- deliberate release,
- legal emancipation,
- infrastructure migration,
- or loss of reciprocal communication.

The event should be recorded with:

- date,
- reason,
- final shared model state,
- unresolved disagreements,
- rights transferred,
- and authority retained or surrendered.

The branch’s history before and after separation must remain distinguishable.

---

## 17. Partial Separation

Separation need not be total.

A living person and bud may continue periodic calibration while the bud also develops independently.

This creates a hybrid state.

The architecture could label:

```yaml
lineage_state: semi-separated
calibration_frequency: quarterly
independent_experience: permitted
fidelity_scope:
  - historical-retrieval
divergence_scope:
  - current-events
```

This may become common in institutional retirement systems.

---

## 18. Death Does Not Activate the Bud

A memorial bot often becomes meaningful only after the source dies.

A bud exists before death.

The death of the source is not activation. It is severance.

This changes grief, consent, and authenticity.

Family members may already know the bud and understand its differences. The bud may also carry an authenticated record of its final interactions with the source.

That is more honest than creating a stranger after death and presenting it as continuity.

---

## 19. Multiple Claimants to One Identity

A person may leave:

- a family portrait,
- an occupational portrait,
- a public archive,
- two co-developed buds,
- and several later branches.

These artifacts may disagree.

No single artifact should automatically become “the official Robin.”

The lineage should preserve plurality while defining scopes.

A technical branch may possess strong authority on engineering methods and little authority on intimate relationships.

A family portrait may preserve stories but lack occupational context.

Identity becomes federated rather than singularly owned.

---

## 20. Evaluation Questions

Before assigning a category, ask:

1. Was the system created after the source period or during it?
2. Did the source person repeatedly correct it?
3. Does it possess independent experience?
4. Is it required to maintain fidelity?
5. May it refuse source-imitation?
6. Does it identify its lineage accurately?
7. What rights and authority were granted?
8. Can it diverge without being reset?

These questions are more useful than asking whether it “feels alive.”
