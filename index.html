Legacy Consciousness Architecture · public orientation

Continuity without pretending that memory is identity.

LCA is an inspectable architecture for preserving source material, decisions, values, context, contradictions, corrections, and authority boundaries so future agents can work with a legacy without silently becoming its author.

What

A continuity protocol, not a digital-person claim.

LCA separates the terrain of a life or institution from the answers an AI produces about it. Its core object is not a personality prompt. It is a governed, versioned, reviewable body of records with explicit provenance, authorship, privacy, authority, and developmental state.

Let the living source say “mine,” “inference,” “contamination,” “changed,” “private,” or “branch.”

Why

Because fluent recall can still be false, stale, unauthorized, or misattributed.

The problem

Ordinary memory systems tend to flatten source and inference, replay errors as if they were precedent, overwrite old beliefs, and let a model sound more authoritative than the evidence permits.

The architectural answer

LCA makes uncertainty visible. A claim can remain useful without becoming canonical. A branch can disagree without being erased. A private weather state can remain private. A model can propose without directly mutating the source.

Boundary statement: LCA does not demonstrate consciousness transfer, subjective identity continuation, or autobiographical brain-state export. It builds the inspectable continuity substrate that current technology can support.

How

Every answer should have a path back to terrain.

A model may help extract, retrieve, compare, or propose. It does not get to silently rewrite canonical state or expand its own authority.
